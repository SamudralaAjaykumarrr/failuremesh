# FailureMesh: technical review brief

**Purpose:** invite criticism of the proposed architecture and adoption assumptions. This is a planning package, not a product demonstration or evidence that an expert has validated the thesis. Please use public sources and generalized experience. Do not provide credentials, customer secrets, private production logs, personal data, confidential postmortems, proprietary code, or restricted customer information. Raw private evidence is not required.

## Proposal and boundaries

FailureMesh's mission is that no engineering team should have to learn a software failure the industry has already learned. It proposes converting incident mechanisms into portable, privacy-preserving, executable protection. A **Portable Failure Contract (PFC)** is a versioned causal description: prerequisites, controls under test, sequence, forbidden outcome, invariant, abstract experiment, proof needs, safety restrictions, and provenance. It is more than an incident summary or chaos script. An **Architecture Contract (AC)** records scoped, evidenced system facts, including transaction and external-effect boundaries, delivery, retries, leases, controls, and observability; missing or conflicting facts remain unknown.

The proposed matcher evaluates PFC prerequisites against AC facts as `APPLICABLE`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, or `UNKNOWN`. `APPLICABLE` means the causal experiment is meaningful and mappable, not that exposure is predicted. A control such as idempotency is normally tested by execution; its presence alone does not imply `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Decisive absence or a specified evidenced exclusion can establish `NOT_APPLICABLE`; unresolved facts yield `UNKNOWN`. Only an applicable candidate can proceed to experiment compilation and separate safety planning. An approved plan would allow local/customer-controlled execution. A versioned verifier would then issue `EXPOSED`, scoped `PROVEN_RESILIENT`, or `UNKNOWN` from recorded evidence. Resilience must bind the PFC version, build, environment, finite experiment space, invariant, complete observations, and safety controls. No universal immunity claim follows.

AI may suggest mechanisms, AC facts, experiments, and summaries; it cannot invent observations, relax proof requirements, authorize unsafe execution, or issue verdicts. Raw customer source, logs, identifiers, traces, secrets, and private incidents remain local by default. Only explicitly approved, sufficiently generalized structures may be shared. Execution has a safety ceiling; production fault injection is never the default and would require explicit bounded authorization. These are specified boundaries, not implemented capabilities.

## Current corpus and gate

The ten canonical families are: (1) timeout after external commit; (2) duplicate queue delivery; (3) stale worker after lease expiry; (4) retry amplification/retry storm; (5) PostgreSQL connection-pool exhaustion; (6) poison-message retry loop; (7) partial transaction/business-state divergence; (8) dependency latency cascade; (9) cache stampede; (10) worker crash after durable write before acknowledgement.

**CURRENT FACT:** Phase 0 is complete. Gate A is **REVISE**. All ten families have exact-family **L0 mechanism-fit mappings**, but only **8/10** have qualifying public observed-incident evidence. Family 1 currently has injected response-loss evidence, not a qualifying observed incident; family 3 has technical race analysis, not a sufficiently observed incident sequence. Both remain unresolved. The final generic incident-search pass is closed. The baseline requires ten qualifying incidents and an explicit new gate decision before Phase 1; therefore **Phase 1 is NOT AUTHORIZED**. No FailureMesh product implementation, incident reproduction, customer applicability verdict, execution verdict, portability proof, expert validation, or market validation exists.

**HYPOTHESIS TO TEST:** causal mechanisms can transfer across organizations, ACs can capture decisive facts without exposing raw data, applicability can earn trust, and safe local experiments can produce useful scoped evidence. Future validation would need executable PFCs, reproducible experiments, cross-architecture evidence, technical interviews, historical-incident reproduction, and eventual adoption evidence in the [baseline's sequence](../company/canonical-baseline-v1.0.md). This review tests assumptions; it does not complete any of those milestones.

**Review request:** identify concrete counterexamples, unavailable architecture facts, false-positive or false-negative failure modes, unacceptable execution or evidence requirements, and reasons a team would decline adoption.
