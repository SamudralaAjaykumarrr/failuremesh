## Reconsideration trigger and evidence handling

Gate A may be **reconsidered for validation completion only** when (a) a concrete candidate public source is presented that plausibly supplies missing exact-family observed-incident evidence for family 1 or 3, or (b) a separately approved baseline change revises that validation rule. The bounded-entry approval alone does not complete Gate A or trigger GO. A mere idea to search, another mechanism demonstration, or elapsed time is not a trigger. Contradictory evidence about an already counted incident or a material abstraction defect must be recorded and assessed promptly for checkpoint integrity or STOP; it may require a corrective Gate A decision without supplying the missing incident.

For a presented family-1 or family-3 source, review the original source, provenance, and source class before touching the checkpoint. Establish that it documents a **real observed operational failure**, not only a synthetic run, formal model, code-path hypothesis, or advice. Map the observed chronology to the defining family mechanism at the exact-family level; identify the committed effect and post-commit caller uncertainty followed by retry and a second committed logical effect for family 1, or lease/authority expiry or takeover followed by an observed stale actor's action for family 3. Do not infer an unreported step from a plausible architecture. Record the scope of observation, ambiguity, and any missing chronology, causal link, business effect, or production status as `UNKNOWN`. Check whether the incident is independent and eligible for one previously missing family slot, rather than double-counting one event or re-labeling a near-match. Compare it with the existing incident record and [abstraction-fit review](abstraction-fit.md). Only a documented, reviewed update to the relevant evidence record, classification, fit audit, and Gate A decision may change the count or gate; presentation alone changes neither.

Technical analysis, an injected or mechanism-only reproduction, a family-name match, a near-match lacking the defining sequence, provider guidance, an unsupported retrospective inference, an LLM suggestion, and customer applicability or hypothetical business impact do **not** justify GO or count as the missing incident. An observed incident may have limitations, but those limitations must stay visible; `UNKNOWN` is not silently converted to fact. No FailureMesh L1 reproduction, portability, or execution verdict follows from a public incident.

## Decision rule

The table describes responses to new validation information; every change to the recorded checkpoint requires an explicit reviewed decision. “No authorization” means no **additional** implementation authority beyond the approved bounded prototype.

GO requires a reviewed corpus of ten **qualifying public observed incidents**, one for each canonical family, encoded as L0 candidates; an updated fit review showing the PFC abstraction represents their materially different mechanisms without forced mappings; and three specified conceptual reference architecture shapes. Source limits and unresolved facts must remain explicit. A new authoritative Gate A decision must record that these conditions hold. The existing 10/10 mechanism mappings, three conceptual shapes, and bounded prototype authorization do not overcome the current 8/10 incident result.

REVISE remains required if either missing family still lacks qualifying incident evidence, any counted incident loses eligibility, a mapping depends on invented causal facts, or a material fit concern remains unresolved but potentially repairable within the baseline. If evidence shows a defining mechanism cannot be represented honestly by the current PFC/AC abstraction, or that core privacy, safety, evidence, or verifier requirements cannot be maintained, stop progression and document architectural reconsideration. A STOP conclusion requires its own reasoned decision; it does not silently amend the baseline or authorize a different product. Missing evidence alone is REVISE, not proof of architectural failure.

Any proposed thesis or milestone change must be separately labeled **PROPOSED BASELINE CHANGE** and state what changes, why, supporting evidence, the difference from the current baseline, and why the benefit justifies changing the thesis. This document proposes no such change. Until explicit approval, the existing baseline and ten-incident milestone govern; even after approval, a new gate/phase decision is required.
