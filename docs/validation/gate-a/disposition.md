## Reconsideration trigger and evidence handling

Gate A may be **reconsidered only** when (a) a concrete candidate public source is presented that plausibly supplies missing exact-family observed-incident evidence for family 1 or 3, or (b) an explicitly approved **PROPOSED BASELINE CHANGE** exists. A mere idea to search, another mechanism demonstration, or elapsed time is not a trigger. Contradictory evidence about an already counted incident or a material abstraction defect must be recorded and assessed promptly for checkpoint integrity or STOP, but it does not itself supply the missing incident or license Phase 1. It may require a corrective Gate A decision even while the reopening trigger for GO remains unmet.

For a presented family-1 or family-3 source, review the original source, provenance, and source class before touching the checkpoint. Establish that it documents a **real observed operational failure**, not only a synthetic run, formal model, code-path hypothesis, or advice. Map the observed chronology to the defining family mechanism at the exact-family level; identify the committed effect and post-commit caller uncertainty followed by retry and a second committed logical effect for family 1, or lease/authority expiry or takeover followed by an observed stale actor's action for family 3. Do not infer an unreported step from a plausible architecture. Record the scope of observation, ambiguity, and any missing chronology, causal link, business effect, or production status as `UNKNOWN`. Check whether the incident is independent and eligible for one previously missing family slot, rather than double-counting one event or re-labeling a near-match. Compare it with the existing incident record and [abstraction-fit review](abstraction-fit.md). Only a documented, reviewed update to the relevant evidence record, classification, fit audit, and Gate A decision may change the count or gate; presentation alone changes neither.

Technical analysis, an injected or mechanism-only reproduction, a family-name match, a near-match lacking the defining sequence, provider guidance, an unsupported retrospective inference, an LLM suggestion, and customer applicability or hypothetical business impact do **not** justify GO or count as the missing incident. An observed incident may have limitations, but those limitations must stay visible; `UNKNOWN` is not silently converted to fact. No FailureMesh L1 reproduction, portability, or execution verdict follows from a public incident.

## Decision rule

The table describes the authorized response to new information; every change to the recorded checkpoint requires an explicit reviewed decision. “No authorization” means Phase 1 stays NOT AUTHORIZED.

| Trigger | Allowed action | Gate A consequence | Phase 1 consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qualifying exact-family observed incident for family 1 only | Review and encode its source-limited evidence; reassess fit and count | Still REVISE at most 9/10 while family 3 is missing | No authorization |
| Qualifying exact-family observed incident for family 3 only | Review and encode its source-limited evidence; reassess fit and count | Still REVISE at most 9/10 while family 1 is missing | No authorization |
| Qualifying exact-family observed evidence for both missing families | Review each independently, update eligible records and fit audit, then make a new Gate A decision | GO is possible only if all entry conditions below hold; otherwise REVISE or STOP | Only an explicit GO/entry authorization permits Phase 1 |
| Another mechanism-only reproduction | Retain as L0 mechanism context if relevant | REVISE; no incident-count increase | No authorization |
| Technical analysis without observed incident | Record limits as mechanism context if relevant | REVISE; no incident-count increase | No authorization |
| Near-match incident | Document why the exact sequence is unsupported; retain `UNKNOWN` | REVISE; no incident-count increase | No authorization |
| Contradictory evidence weakening an existing counted incident | Audit provenance and eligibility; correct record, count, and decision if warranted | REVISE with corrected count, or STOP if a deeper blocker is established; never silently retain 8/10 | No authorization |
| Evidence that a current PFC abstraction is materially wrong | Reassess fit and baseline compatibility; document blocker | REVISE while repairable within baseline; STOP/architectural reconsideration if the core mapping cannot be made honestly | No authorization |
| Explicitly approved baseline change | Apply its approved scope through change control and make a new Gate A decision | Reassess under approved rules; approval alone is not GO | Only a later explicit entry authorization permits Phase 1 |
| No new evidence | Preserve checkpoint; perform only allowed preparatory work | REVISE, 10/10 mechanism fit and 8/10 incident coverage | No authorization |

GO requires a reviewed corpus of ten **qualifying public observed incidents**, one for each canonical family, encoded as L0 candidates; an updated fit review showing the PFC abstraction represents their materially different mechanisms without forced mappings; and three specified conceptual reference architecture shapes. Source limits and unresolved facts must remain explicit. A new authoritative Gate A decision must record that these conditions hold and explicitly authorize Phase 1 entry. The existing 10/10 mechanism mappings and three conceptual shapes do not overcome the current 8/10 incident result.

REVISE remains required if either missing family still lacks qualifying incident evidence, any counted incident loses eligibility, a mapping depends on invented causal facts, or a material fit concern remains unresolved but potentially repairable within the baseline. If evidence shows a defining mechanism cannot be represented honestly by the current PFC/AC abstraction, or that core privacy, safety, evidence, or verifier requirements cannot be maintained, stop progression and document architectural reconsideration. A STOP conclusion requires its own reasoned decision; it does not silently amend the baseline or authorize a different product. Missing evidence alone is REVISE, not proof of architectural failure.

Any proposed thesis or milestone change must be separately labeled **PROPOSED BASELINE CHANGE** and state what changes, why, supporting evidence, the difference from the current baseline, and why the benefit justifies changing the thesis. This document proposes no such change. Until explicit approval, the existing baseline and ten-incident milestone govern; even after approval, a new gate/phase decision is required.
