# Corpus selection, exposure and freeze protocol

Status: **proposed Phase 2B protocol; no actual selection or registration**. Implements the curation portion of the [evaluation protocol](evaluation-protocol.md), using the [record model](corpus-record-model.md). Later evidence extraction, architecture packets, matching, grading and execution remain separately authorized.

## Register the selection policy first

The purpose is difficult, auditable testing of causal failure transfer across independently documented systems. This is a falsification corpus, not a statistical population sample, searchable incident database, postmortem-summary collection, leaderboard, LLM training corpus or chaos catalog.

A selection-round policy records its ID/version, five-family vocabulary, scope, manual discovery channels, start/cutoff, finite search effort budget per family (candidate/curator-hour limits supplied before collection), eligibility rules, challenge coverage, target counts, publisher constraint, split algorithm, seed or explicit no-randomness, rights requirements, exposure exclusions, stop rules and tool identity. No default unlimited search. Register the policy before candidate decisions; subsequent changes retain parent, reasons and exposure. Stop at the budget/cutoff and show deficits. This design supplies no acquisition authority or real search budget.

Target approximately **25 natural public incidents across approximately five materially different causal families**, about five origins per family, and conceptually **15 development / 5 validation / 5 holdout**, about 3/1/1 per family. These targets remain hypotheses about feasibility. A small deficient freeze is allowed only with explicit shortfall status; it cannot be reported as target completion. The separate 20–30 architecture-packet target is unchanged and outside this phase.

## Manual selection procedure

1. Register each submitted candidate before judging usefulness; record all known prior exposure and discovery basis. Intake can fail structurally or on privacy grounds, but a safe attempt event remains. Tool completeness is bounded to submitted attempts, not undisclosed curator activity.
2. Resolve origin/episode/project and publisher aliases conservatively. Keep duplicate entries with links; choose one natural representative per origin for counting. Do not erase secondary leads or inaccessible sources.
3. Record a vector of evidence directness, causal ordering, mechanism detail, limitations, recoverable provenance, architecture context and reproduction potential. Prefer first-party observations and independent architecture documentation. Separate reported runtime facts from design documents. Vendor fame and prose polish have no numeric weight.
4. Record tentative family and alternatives before matcher output. Seek ambiguous, version-dependent, cross-boundary, protected and near-match cases as well as clear mechanisms. “Protected” and “near-match” are selection hypotheses, not expected applicability or execution labels. Policy specifies the desired challenge categories; empty categories are deficits.
5. Apply hard boundaries: public natural origin for natural counts; safe permitted metadata; resolved count identity; explicit rights/audit limits; complete exposure and decision records. A metadata-only or weak-evidence case may be retained as a challenge, but report it separately from adequately evidenced candidates. Missing reproduction capability is a limitation, not automatically a rejection or applicability UNKNOWN.
6. Record `INCLUDED`, `DEFERRED` or `EXCLUDED`, reason codes and tradeoffs for every candidate at cutoff. PENDING entries must become DEFERRED before freeze. Reasons include `DUPLICATE`, `ORIGIN_UNRESOLVED`, `WEAK_EVIDENCE`, `CAUSAL_AMBIGUITY`, `ARCHITECTURE_CONTEXT_MISSING`, `INACCESSIBLE`, `RIGHTS_BLOCKED`, `PRIVACY_QUARANTINE`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, `SYNTHETIC_ONLY`, `PUBLISHER_CONCENTRATION`, `CAPACITY_LIMIT`, `PRIOR_RESULT_EXPOSURE`, `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`; multiple reasons may apply. Reopening requires a revision.
7. Close the complete candidate inventory and decisions, allocate eligible units, then freeze the full manifest before results. Selection rationale must explain alternatives and difficult-family shortfalls, not merely list successful entries. An informed case can remain development material without masquerading as a prospective test.

Report raw submissions, rejected attempts, natural representatives, duplicate aliases, synthetic entries, unresolved origins, tentative family totals, adequate/limited evidence, audit tiers, project/vendor/publisher concentration, split totals and all exclusions by candidate ID. No one scalar “quality” score. Do not interpret the approximately 25 target as 25 adequately evidenced independent cases if only some qualify.

Publisher cap: default `3 × selected natural representatives from one normalized publisher group <= total selected natural representatives` (at 25, at most 8). Report raw and grouped names and unresolved ownership. Unknown publishers cannot establish diversity and block a cap-compliant claim. A pre-result policy revision may document an explicit concentration exception and shortfall; display `DIVERSITY_SHORTFALL` permanently for that round, not “diverse” by exception. Also report project and stack concentrations: different products owned by one publisher and renamed/forked projects cannot disguise dependence. No post-result waiver or family substitution can repair original prospective claims.

## Split assignment and exposure eligibility

Allocation uses whole dependency components from the record model, not individual URLs or rows. All members, mirrors, revisions and synthetic descendants share a split. Project lineage is kept together even across distinct episodes. Synthetic members add zero natural cases to quotas. Excluded members retain their component's split history but have `NOT_SELECTED` membership; they cannot be reintroduced elsewhere later.

Freeze an explicit assignment table, justified by the following constraints, rather than build a quota optimizer. The table is a deterministic input. V1 uses no random selection: seed is null with `NOT_USED`, allocation method `explicit-table-v1`. If a later policy uses seeded ordering, pin its exact algorithm/version and seed before decisions; changing seed creates a new round and retains every attempt. No seed shopping after outcomes.

Fresh validation/holdout eligibility requires no prior development/validation use of the component, no known target-PFC, matcher-rule, expected-result, prior-case-outcome or previous-evaluation-result access by its selectors/assigners, and documented role/context restrictions for subsequent evaluation. UNKNOWN access in these dimensions blocks a fresh claim. Curators may inspect incident content and tentative family labels and record that access; family-stratified selection is inherently family-informed. Architecture-packet access and unavoidable outcome cues must also be disclosed. This is prospective withholding, not a blanket claim of blindness.

At freeze, list freshness per component: `ELIGIBLE_UNREVEALED`, `EXPOSED`, `UNKNOWN`. “Eligible” is scoped to recorded access controls and self-reports; prior LLM training knowledge is always fundamentally UNKNOWN and must appear beside it. A different agent, file, prompt or model is not evidence of withheld information. If selectors know matcher rules from repository work, fresh validation/holdout construction needs a later documented separation arrangement or shows a shortfall; do not hide that constraint to achieve 5/5.

Development can never be promoted to fresh validation/holdout; validation can never become fresh holdout. A holdout may be exposed for its single later registered reveal; subsequently it is regression/development material in new manifests. Historical split remains HOLDOUT in its original manifest with appended exposure. New revisions, new IDs, corrected clusters, new models and new rounds cannot reset exposure inherited from the origin/project/derivative component. Exposed holdout corrections cannot be substituted for the original test.

## Exposure ledger

Each append event binds event ID, sequence, previous event digest, actor ID/class/role, model/version if applicable, context/session ID, affected candidate/component/round, exposure category, access state (`YES`, `NO_WITH_BASIS`, `UNKNOWN`), access basis and restrictions, reported occurrence time (possibly unknown), recorded UTC time, and artifact IDs/digests where known. No timestamp can negate a prior YES. In this ledger, prior case outcome means an evaluation/reproduction outcome used for tuning; the reported outage outcome read during incident curation is separately disclosed as an unavoidable source cue. Otherwise reading an incident would falsely imply a prior matcher run. Events also cover discovery, selection, assignment, freeze, reveal, attempted prohibited mutations, corrections, invalidation and withdrawal.

Required categories for every involved curator/tool/model context: target PFC, matcher rules, expected result, prior case outcome, architecture packet, causal-family label, previous benchmark/evaluation result. Record actual access and available access separately; a shared readable workspace is available access even if an actor claims not to have opened a file. Unknown context inheritance is UNKNOWN, never NO. Capture prompt/context manifests only when permitted; safe identities/reasons suffice if raw context is sensitive. Model pretraining exposure is a separate immutable UNKNOWN disclosure, not a negative declaration.

Effective exposure is the conservative union across all relevant contexts, revisions, aliases and ancestors. YES dominates UNKNOWN, which dominates NO_WITH_BASIS. Access to target/rules/results by selectors makes their selection matcher-informed. Record that limitation even if no explicit match was run. Newly discovered pre-freeze exposure appends a correction and invalidates fresh eligibility retroactively for current reporting; old manifest bytes remain. The local tool cannot detect lies, withheld external runs or unrecorded access and must state this limit.

## Freeze and registration lifecycle

`DRAFT → SELECTED → FROZEN → EXPOSED → RETIRED`, with revision links to a new draft/manifest. A frozen unexposed round may also retire due to correction or withdrawal. Selection is revisable by append-only candidate revisions until freeze. EXPOSED is a monotonic ledger property of components, not a writable candidate boolean. Retirement never erases membership or failures.

Freeze requires a registered selection policy, a closed inventory through a named ledger sequence/head, every candidate's final decision, resolved references or explicit uncaptured citations, cluster constraints, completed exposure matrix, current rights checks at supplied policy time, explicit assignment table and all deviations. Freezing does not require meeting 25/5/15/5/5, but must output `SHORTFALL` and the exact deficit vector if unmet. It fails for hidden exclusions, split conflicts, invalid references, misrepresented fresh status or prohibited retention. It never automatically substitutes an easier family or promotes a weak source.

Write immutable objects, verify their closure, publish the manifest, then append a freeze receipt referring to it. The manifest binds the pre-freeze ledger head; the receipt binds the manifest, avoiding a hash cycle. Under one local writer lock, a receipt is the commit point; interrupted object writes leave unregistered objects, never a successful freeze. A retry of the same operation ID is idempotent. Recovery must report orphan objects/pending attempts and cannot silently replace a committed receipt. Every operation checks the expected ledger head; concurrent stale writers fail explicitly.

The receipt records local sequence, parent event digest, manifest digest, actor/tool and supplied reported freeze time. Local-only registration is **self-reported chronology**; ordered writes are inspectable within the tool but Phase 2B enforces no matcher write-before-run linkage. It must not claim the later evaluation protocol's locally enforced pre-registration tier, independent chronology, independent reviewer approval or blind evaluation. Hashes establish bytes only. A later evaluator must separately register extraction/packet/PFC/pairing/procedure inputs before running. A corpus freeze is necessary selection provenance, not complete evaluation registration.

Post-freeze corrections create new candidate/cluster/policy revisions and a new corpus version with parent-manifest digest, change reasons and exposure inheritance. Original decisions, splits, receipts and later outcomes remain addressable. Current verification checks both the frozen historical snapshot and all later local notices through an explicit current ledger head. Supplying an old head yields historical-only inspection, never a claim of current admissibility or freshness. A locally rewritable administrator can replace the entire history; detection requires an independently retained head, which this phase does not provide.
